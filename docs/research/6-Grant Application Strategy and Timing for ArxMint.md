# Grant Application Strategy and Timing for ArxMint

## Landscape summary and evaluation lens

ArxMint’s grant fit is strongest where funders explicitly value (a) Bitcoin-adjacent “freedom tech” infrastructure, (b) privacy and ecash, and (c) open-source public goods shipped in the open with concrete milestones and reporting. OpenSats’ published criteria (“Good for Bitcoin,” “Free and Open-Source,” “Transparency & Education”) and their active ecash support make them a primary target. citeturn7view1turn10view3

HRF’s Bitcoin Development Fund is also unusually aligned because it explicitly funds ecash and “Bitcoin utility applications,” alongside privacy/security/decentralization work and community-building, with year-round intake and quarterly announcements. citeturn23view0turn23view2

Spiral (the evolution of Square Crypto) sits between “protocol” and “application layer”: it has historically stated it funds free/open-source projects that improve Bitcoin across UX, scaling, privacy, and security, and their public project roster includes BTCPay Server (merchant acceptance) and multiple developer toolkits—clear precedent for application-layer grants. citeturn22view0turn20view0

By contrast, Brink and Maelstrom are best understood as “Bitcoin protocol engineer funding” rather than “stack-integrator product grants.” Brink describes year-long grants to established protocol developers with a security/stability focus, and their historical full-time contracts emphasize exclusivity (no additional sponsor funding during the grant term). citeturn19view1turn19view3 Maelstrom’s program similarly frames expectations around direct technical contributions (e.g., Bitcoin Core PRs/review), even while allowing grant stacking up to a yearly cap. citeturn6search28

FBCE/Bitcoin Beach Grants are the best match for the **Longmont pilot** specifically (community traction and circular economy proof-of-work), but they are *not* designed to underwrite deep protocol/tooling R&D. Their published winners list shows “small grants” in sats allocated across many local initiatives, which is strategically useful for pilot ops, on-the-ground onboarding, and demonstrable loop metrics. citeturn17search1turn17search3

## KPIs that make ArxMint competitive for grants

Because grantmakers in this space are funding public goods, the “best KPIs” are the ones that reduce perceived execution risk and demonstrate durable ecosystem impact. For ArxMint, a competitive KPI set should combine:

**Open-source infrastructure KPIs** (best for OpenSats/Spiral/HRF):
- Number of reproducible deployments created (Fedimint federations + Cashu mints + L402 service templates) with versioned config manifests.
- Security posture milestones (e.g., documented security tiers, threat models, reproducible builds, automated integration tests, deployment hardening).
- Developer adoption: unique deployers, repeat deployers, downstream projects using the generated templates, and maintainer responsiveness (issues closed, release cadence).
- Privacy capabilities shipped and documented (e.g., spend-routing policy options, “silent payments support” if applicable, and clear operational guidance—especially where user safety is at stake). These align with funder priorities around privacy/security and practical deployability. citeturn7view1turn23view0turn22view0

**Circular economy KPIs** (best for FBCE/Bitcoin Beach):
- Active merchants accepting sats (your target of 30 merchants is directionally aligned with how circular economies are usually evaluated).
- Monthly active users transacting (your 300 MAU target is a legitimate anchor).
- Repeat-spend depth: percent of users who transact again within 30 days; merchant re-spend (merchants paying suppliers/employees in sats).
- Bitcoin-denominated pricing behaviors (use of BTC as unit of account is explicitly referenced in FBCE’s tier framework). citeturn17search0turn24search8  
- Operational education metrics: number of merchant trainings/onboardings, number of support touchpoints, and time-to-first-transaction.

A practical way to make the Longmont targets “grant-credible” is to lock them to quarterly milestones because major grant programs use quarterly reporting/announcement cycles. OpenSats’ reporting cadence is explicitly monthly early-on for first-time grantees and then quarterly. citeturn12view2 HRF announces quarterly. citeturn23view0turn23view1
